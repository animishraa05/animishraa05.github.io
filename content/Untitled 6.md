**Caveats on this workload that no spec sheet captures:**

- **Discord on mobile is the weak link on every platform.** Its mobile screen-share encoder and quality tiers are more limited than desktop; expect roughly 720p–1080p at 30fps regardless of tablet, and check the current Discord client for what your account tier allows.
- **On iPadOS, screen broadcast runs through a memory-capped system extension**, which is reliable but is the reason Discord share on an iPad is stable and modest rather than sharp.
- **Camera recording while screen-sharing** is the load spike. It is what separates 12GB+ flagship chips from 8GB/mid-range ones.

### 3.2 How 8GB vs 12GB vs 16GB behaves on this load (my engineering estimate, not measured)

|Resident memory item (Android 16 class tablet)|Typical working set|
|---|---|
|OS + system UI + large-screen/windowing services|2.8–3.8 GB|
|Notes app with ink layer + stylus services|0.5–0.9 GB|
|YouTube in split window (1080p/4K decode surfaces)|0.6–1.0 GB|
|Discord (voice + screen-capture encode)|0.9–1.4 GB|
|Camera/recorder app (encode buffers)|0.7–1.2 GB|
|**Live working set**|**≈ 5.5–8.3 GB**|
|Cached apps you want to keep warm (browser, LMS, chat)|+1.5–3 GB|

- **8GB:** after kernel/reserved memory you have ~6.5–7GB usable. The live set consumes all of it, so Android's low-memory killer evicts cached apps first, then the **most recently backgrounded heavy app (usually Discord or the notes app) when the camera opens**. ZRAM compression buys ~1.5–2.5GB of effective space but costs CPU during encode. "RAM Plus"/virtual RAM on Samsung uses storage, is slower and adds flash wear. **Expect reloads.** On iPadOS 8GB (M3), jetsam suspends then terminates backgrounded apps, so the same pattern appears; iPadOS handles it more gracefully but Discord's call state can drop.
- **12GB:** ~10–10.5GB usable. The whole workload fits with **2–4GB of cache** for 2–3 warm background apps. **This is the realistic minimum for "no reloads" in years 1–3.**
- **16GB:** ~14GB usable. Everything plus 5–8 cached apps. For _this_ workload the jump from 12→16GB is marginal in year 1 and matters in years 4–5 when apps and the OS grow. It does not rescue a slow SoC or a short support window.
- **iPad nuance:** M4 + 12GB is also the tier Apple gates its heaviest on-device Apple Intelligence features to; the 8GB M3 Air runs iPadOS 27 but is classed "mostly compatible".

### 4.2 Hardware aging: Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 vs Apple M3/M4 vs Exynos 1580

Relative single-chip performance index, **Exynos 1580 = 1.0** (rounded ballparks from background benchmark knowledge, not measured here†): Dimensity 7300 ≈ 0.9 · **Exynos 1580 = 1.0** · SD 8s Gen 4 ≈ 1.9 · **SD 8 Gen 3 ≈ 2.0** · SD 8 Elite ≈ 2.8 · **Apple M3 ≈ 3.4** · SD 8 Elite Gen 5 ≈ 3.4 · **Apple M4 ≈ 3.9**.

- **Exynos 1580 (Galaxy FE series):** adequate for notes + YouTube today, **marginal for the full simultaneous workload today**, and the one most likely to feel slow by 2028–29 as apps grow. Its saving grace is **7 years of OS/security**, so it stays _safe_ and _current_ long after it stops being _quick_. This is a "long runway, low ceiling" chip.
- **Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (Lenovo):** a 2023 flagship that will remain comfortably adequate for notes/video through ~2029–30. Its problem is not speed, it is **support: patches end ~Jan 2029**, after which banking/enterprise/DRM apps increasingly refuse old patch levels. Speed outlives support here.
- **Apple M3/M4:** silicon far exceeds anything this workload needs through 2031, so aging is dominated by **RAM (8GB on M3 is the ceiling around 2029+; 12GB on M4 holds)**, battery, and price. The M4's 12GB plus Apple's long update history makes it the lowest-risk 5-year machine, at 1.3–2× the money.
- **Snapdragon 8 Elite / Elite Gen 5 / 8s Gen 4 (OnePlus, Xiaomi):** fastest Android chips here; with 12GB+ they stay quick to 2030. Support (3–4 OS, 6 yrs patches) just clears 2031.

**Non-silicon aging, all tablets:** Li-ion cells typically fall to ~80% capacity in 500–1,000 full cycles (Apple rates iPads at 1,000). Daily heavy use means **plan on a battery replacement around year 3–4**; Samsung and Apple have the widest service coverage in India, OnePlus/Xiaomi/Lenovo thinner.
